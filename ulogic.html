uLogic. The commercial logic behind digital infrastructure.

uLogic builds the commercial platform for data center and cloud infrastructure businesses: quoting, provisioning, renewals and margin, in one system. Its team has run global data center platforms, and the product has to carry the same depth without the complexity showing.

Led and owned the product design at A Small Studio®: the renewal workflows, account and project views, and the design system underneath them, from flows and states through to dev-ready screens.

Renewals Are the Business

For an infrastructure operator, most revenue comes back around as renewals: colocation, interconnects and cloud contracts, each with its own term, notice period, pricing and escalation. Missing a date or under-pricing a renewal costs real margin. The work was to turn that into a workspace an account manager can move through quickly and trust.

Find the Account That Needs You

The Renewal Designer opens on all accounts, with total MRR, the owner and a risk flag on each, so the ones that need attention surface first. Search covers accounts and customers, and filters narrow by team member, region and MRR. Applied filters stay in view as chips above the list, so it’s always clear what’s being shown.

From Every Account to Every Line

An account opens on its MRR by type, then MRR by project, grouped by how soon each renews, from month to month out to a year. A project shows the same summary at the top and every order line underneath, grouped by order, with the fields that matter for a renewal side by side and problems flagged where they sit.

The account page grew into one view of everything at stake: the customers under their ultimate customer, each renewal project with an owner and a health score, and every service sorted by risk, with the money behind each tier. A project’s information tab does the same for its own services, counting what still needs validating and showing each customer’s progress.

Expiry at a Glance

The date a service expires decides how urgent its renewal is, so the field carries it. No date, more than six months, inside three months, inside thirty days and expired each get their own colour, and hovering says exactly how long is left.

Many Lines at Once

Renewals rarely change one line at a time. Selecting order lines, across orders, brings up a bulk edit, so a new term or price goes onto every selected line in one step. When a long order needs scanning instead, a flatter view puts each line on a single row.

Every Change, Reviewed Before It Saves

Before anything is saved, View changes lists every edited service with its original values in grey and the new ones in black. A service with child services shows how many, and their changes appear beneath it only when there are some. The panel grows with its content up to a set height, then scrolls with its headers pinned, so a long review never loses its place.

Approvals and Denials, Mapped

Renewals pass between account managers and the renewal desk, so every route was designed through, from each side. A submitted project waits for review with Approve and Deny at the top. While it’s under review it can’t be changed. Approved, it goes straight on to a quote. Denying it asks for the reason and what to fix, and that note arrives on the project as a comment.

A System Before More Screens

Moving fast had left the baseline components half-built. The fix was to step back and rebuild from the bottom: colour primitives first, then semantic tokens for background, text, icon and border, then stackable base components, and only then the product components. A change to the system now happens once, in the variables, without breaking the screens built on it.

Decisions

  • Risk first. Accounts open sorted by what needs attention, not alphabetically, so the work starts where the money is at stake.
  • Validate in place. Every order line can be checked and validated where it sits. No separate review screen, and nothing gets lost between steps.
  • Review before save. Every edit is shown against its original before it’s accepted, so a bulk change can’t slip through unseen.
  • One scroll at a time. The page and the service chart used to scroll against each other. The chart now opens to full screen and locks in place at the bottom of the page, so it scrolls on its own.
  • Variables before components. Rebuilding the system from primitives up slowed the next screens down, and made every screen after them faster and safer to change.

Outcome

Shipped to production in early 2026, saving account teams an average of 10 hours a week on renewals.
